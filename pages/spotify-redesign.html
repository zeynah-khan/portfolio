Spotify redesign

Spotify's recommendations are among the best in streaming, but its homepage buries them. This redesign gives every section a clear heading and lets people pin, sort and rearrange their homepage around what they actually listen to.

The redesign

Three changes, one goal

Make Spotify's best feature, personalisation, easy to find and easy to shape.

01

Sections that say what they are

Content sits under bold headings like "Made For You" and "Based On Your Recent Listening", so personalised picks stand out from generic tiles. In Music, a Discover view gives new releases their own sections.

02

Pin what matters to the top

Every section has a menu. Choosing Pin moves that section to the top of the homepage, and a label confirms the change straight away, so the page starts with what you care about most.

03

Sort, rearrange or reset

Sort orders a section by recents, recently added or a custom order. Reorganise adds drag handles so sections can be moved up or down. Reset to Default undoes everything in one click.

01

Background

Spotify had over 500 million users when I did this project. It competes with Apple Music, YouTube Music and Amazon Music, and its real edge is personalisation: recommendation algorithms that learn what each person listens to.

But the homepage, the first thing everyone sees, makes that edge hard to notice. I wanted to find out why, and what a homepage built around personalisation could look like.

02

Business analysis

I started with where Spotify stands as a business, using a Business Model Canvas and a SWOT analysis.

Strengths

  • Advanced, personalised recommendation algorithms
  • A wide library of music, podcasts and audiobooks

Weaknesses

  • A cluttered homepage with inconsistent organisation
  • Little user control over what's shown

Opportunities

  • More engagement through customisation
  • Better accessibility to reach more users

Threats

  • Strong competition from Apple Music, YouTube Music and Amazon Music
  • Rising licensing costs

A User-Centred Design canvas then put the people at the centre. It showed two very different groups to design for, with fears that pull in opposite directions:

Users

  • Casual listeners who want a simple platform
  • People who want advanced customisation

Motives

  • Quick access to relevant content
  • A personalised, enjoyable experience

Fears

  • Losing access to favourite content
  • Features that feel complex or overwhelming

Value proposition: a fully customisable, user-focused homepage that makes discovery simpler while still serving personalised recommendations.

03

Evaluating the current homepage

I reviewed the desktop homepage across the Music, Podcasts and Audiobooks tabs and compared it with complaints on the Spotify Community forums. Three problems kept coming up:

  1. Poor organisation

    Recommendations are buried beneath unrelated tiles, so people scroll past the thing Spotify does best.

  2. Lack of clarity

    Small taglines like "Trending Now" don't guide you, so personal and generic content blur together.

  3. Limited customisation

    Everyone gets the same layout, whether they're there for music, podcasts or audiobooks.

Accessibility suffers too: small text and inconsistent content sizes make the page harder to use for people with low vision.

Usability questions

  • Can people find personalised recommendations without scrolling too far?
  • Are the Music, Podcasts and Audiobooks tabs clear?
  • Is content grouped in a way that reduces cognitive load?

Experience questions

  • Does the homepage feel tailored to the person using it?
  • Are personalised recommendations visually prominent?
  • Is browsing enjoyable rather than frustrating?

04

Defining the goal

How might Spotify's homepage show off its personalisation without overwhelming people who just want to press play?

  1. Simplify navigation

    Group content under distinct subheadings to cut cognitive load.

  2. Make personalisation visible

    Put recommendations where people can't miss them, with a clear visual hierarchy.

  3. Give people control

    Let them pin, sort and rearrange sections, without making customisation compulsory.

05

From a stream to a magazine

I compared the conceptual models behind the current homepage with the ones I wanted to replace them with. Both new models are familiar from other apps, so the structure shouldn't need learning.

Now

Homepage as a stream

  • Effortless scrolling and exploration
  • Simple, predictable navigation
  • Irrelevant content mixed in with personal picks
  • No control over what comes first
Now

Tabs as categories

  • Easy to separate music, podcasts and audiobooks
  • Too broad, with no subcategories to help you discover anything new
Redesign

A magazine layout

  • Bold section headings make content easier to scan and find
  • Clearer organisation reduces cognitive load
  • People may need a little time to adjust
Redesign

A customisable dashboard

  • Pinning sections works like arranging widgets on a phone
  • Relevant content comes first, which boosts engagement
  • Some people may find customisation unnecessary

+ benefit − limitation

06

Design principles

I checked the current design against five interaction design principles, and used each gap to shape the redesign.

PrincipleCurrent designRedesign
Visibility"Made For You" is buried in the stream, and taglines like "Trending Now" are too small to noticeBold subheadings group content and make recommendations obvious
FeedbackVery little feedback when you interactA clear confirmation when you pin, sort or reorganise a section
ConsistencyBranding is consistent, but organisation differs between tabsThe same headings and section menu across every tab, keeping Spotify's branding
ConstraintsNo customisation at allCustomisation within limits: essential sections like "Made For You" can't be removed
AffordanceFew cues about what you can doClear icons, drag handles and distinct buttons for each action

07

Interaction details

Each control is a two-step action from the section menu, and the label in the corner always shows what's been applied.

08

Reflection and next steps

The strongest part of this project is the thinking underneath it. The redesign comes from Spotify's business position and real user complaints, not just opinions about what looks cluttered.

Customisation has trade-offs. Some people won't want it, and a new structure takes time to get used to. That's why the default layout has to work well on its own, and why Reset to Default is always one click away.

  1. Test discoverability

    Do people find Pin and Reorganise without being shown where they are?

  2. Question Reorganise

    Does it add enough value to justify the extra complexity?

  3. Check the headings

    Do they read as helpful structure, or just more text to scan past?

  4. Go responsive

    Design how the layout adapts to tablet and mobile without losing clarity.
